R6-A

Studio

decided 2026-11-23 · with D's tint as a toggle

An asset-store look: a neutral grey studio backdrop whatever the app theme, soft key light with a real shadow on an invisible floor, a faint grid. Controls float over the model in a dark pill; the animation transport is a video-player bar along the bottom; stats sit in a chip above it.

Good

  • Materials and textures read truest: neutral grey shows colour without a theme tint.
  • The transport is familiar from every video player.
  • Nothing outside the viewport: it fits a narrow inspector.

Costs

  • It's about looking at the asset, not at what changed: you have to spot the differences yourself.
  • A grey island in a light-theme app.
  • Overlays cover part of the model on small panes.
R6-B

Inspector panel

not chosen

A tool look: the viewport is bare (app background, crisp grid, axis gizmo), and everything else is a panel on the right, like Unity's own Inspector: the object tree with +/−/~ marks, materials with texture thumbnails, mesh numbers before → after, and the clip list with durations. Click a row to frame that object. Starts in swipe mode here.

Good

  • The most information: every changed object, material and clip is listed, with numbers.
  • Works for big prefabs where a change is hard to see in 3D.
  • Feels like the rest of gitgud (same segment buttons and lists).

Costs

  • The panel eats 290 px of an already split inspector.
  • Busy: two places to look.
  • The 3D view itself doesn't show what changed.
R6-C

Light table

not chosen

Calm and minimal, like the image diff: the backdrop follows the app theme, no grid, just a soft contact shadow; the model turns slowly on a turntable until you touch it. Controls appear only on hover. One caption line under the viewport holds stats, deltas and the clips as chips. Starts in onion mode here: HEAD is the orange ghost.

Good

  • Least chrome; the model has the whole viewport.
  • Matches the image diff's caption and backdrop, so binary previews look like one family.
  • The turntable shows all sides without touching anything.

Costs

  • Hidden controls are harder to discover.
  • Turntable motion can distract while you read the diff.
  • Like A, the view doesn't point at what changed.
R6-D

Change tint

Claude's picknot chosen as the look · tint + legend kept as A's Changes mode

The view answers "what changed?" first. Models open in clay (no textures), tinted by change: green added, red removed, amber modified, grey unchanged. A legend lists each change with why (material swapped, moved 0.36 m); click one to fly to it. "Textured" switches to the real materials. Theme backdrop, faint grid, slim transport at the bottom.

Claude's pick: this is a git tool, so the first question is what changed, and the tint plus the legend answers it before you orbit anything. The textured look is one click away, and the legend carries B's useful part (the change list) without a whole panel.

Good

  • Changes are visible at a glance, even small ones on a big prefab.
  • The legend names each change and flies to it.
  • Clay hides texture noise, so geometry changes stand out.

Costs

  • The first view isn't the real look; a texture-only change shows only as an amber object until you switch.
  • Tint colours have to stay readable on both themes.
  • Needs objects matched by name across versions (a rename reads as removed + added).

A prefab whose mesh or material can't be found still opens: the missing mesh is a magenta wire box at its bounds, the missing material renders magenta, and a warning names each broken reference. It's the same check as the post-merge integrity check.
